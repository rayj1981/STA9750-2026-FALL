What We Watched: Exploring Netflix Viewing Data

Strategy Memo

To: Netflix Leadership
From: Raymond Date: October 2026
Subject: Content Strategy Recommendations for 2027

Dear Netflix Leadership,

As a content strategy consultant, I recommend that Netflix focus its 2027 original-content strategy on globally available television shows while maintaining a broad catalog of content. Netflix should develop and acquire more multi-season shows that can attract viewers across many countries and continue receiving viewing hours over time rather than spending money on creating and acquire movies.

The top 10 titles in 2026H1 accounted for only 6.7% of that period’s total watch time. This shows that Netflix viewers do not spend all of their time watching only a few major hits. Instead, viewing time is spread across many titles in the catalog. Netflix should continue investing in potential blockbuster titles, but it should also maintain a wide selection of shows that can collectively produce steady viewing hours.

Netflix should also make more original titles globally available. Only 24.6% of titles with known availability were globally available, but those titles accounted for 52.1% of total viewing time. This suggests that globally available titles receive substantially more viewing than regionally licensed titles. For 2027, Netflix should prioritize original shows with worldwide rights, international stories, subtitles, dubbing, and marketing that can reach viewers in many territories.

Finally, Netflix should focus more of its original-content budget on television shows. From 2023H2 through 2026H1, shows accounted for approximately 95% of total viewing time in every reporting period. This indicates that viewers spend much more time watching serialized shows than movies. The leading title in every reporting period was also a television show or limited series. For example, The Night Agent: Season 1 led 2023H1, Squid Game: Season 2 led both 2024H2 and 2025H1, and Bridgerton Season 4 led 2026H1. These top titles captured between 0.9% and 1.4% of their period’s viewing hours. This shows that major television series can lead Netflix viewing, while the relatively small shares also show that viewing remains spread across the broader catalog.

Netflix should therefore invest in both potential hit series and a wide selection of returning shows. Netflix should continue producing selected movies, but the largest share of new spending should support shows with multiple seasons and strong potential for repeat viewing. The 10 titles with the most consistently high viewership were all classified as shows. Across all seven reporting periods, titles such as Stranger Things, Suits, and Breaking Bad continued to receive high viewing hours. These results suggest that familiar, multi-season shows can become dependable catalog titles that viewers return to over time. A successful new season can also encourage viewers to return to earlier seasons, creating additional viewing hours for content that Netflix already owns.

For how a season should be made, We suggest that Netflix should prioritize series with strong characters, recognizable settings, and storylines that can continue across several seasons. When a show is renewed, Netflix may be able to reuse sets, props, costumes, visual-effects assets, and experienced production teams. This can make later seasons more efficient to produce.

As For Live events vs Pre-taped content. There were far fewer live-event titles than pre-taped titles: 286 compared with 24,141. The median viewing results were similar, with live events receiving 1.9 million total hours and pre-taped content receiving 1.8 million total hours. This suggests that a typical live event can receive a comparable amount of viewing to a typical pre-taped title.

However, pre-taped content performed much better overall. The average pre-taped title received approximately 21.1 million total viewing hours, compared with 3.9 million for the average live event. The top 10 pre-taped titles averaged about 1.37 billion viewing hours, while the top 10 live events averaged about 37.7 million hours.

Netflix should continue testing a small number of high-profile live events such as the the Paul vs. Tyson fight, especially events that can create marketing attention or attract new subscribers. However, the data suggests that Netflix should keep most of its content investment focused on pre-taped, multi-season shows because they generate much higher viewing hours overall.

Lastly We suggest Netflix focus more on documentaries style shows.

Task 1-3: Loading, Cleaning, Fixing Data

Code 
#' Acquire Netflix What We Watched Reports
#' 
#' This function will acquire and standardize all data for MP#01
#' from Netflix. Because Netflix hosts the data files behind a CDN, we
#' are accessing the release pages and automatically extracting links to the
#' relevant files (with CDN hash). 
#' 
#' After import, files are stacked vertically and some basic cleaning is
#' performed. 
load_netflix_data <- function(){
  # Note to students: Make sure you have installed the tidyverse and fs packages
  #                   before beginning this assignment. If you aren't sure,
  #                   simply run `library(tidyverse)` at your R Console and, if
  #                   you don't see an error message, `tidyverse` has been 
  #                   installed. (Same for `fs`) If you still need to install
  #                   either package, use the `install.packages` function as 
  #                   demonstrated in class.
  library(gt)
  library(skimr)
  library(tidyverse)
  library(rvest)
  library(readxl)
  library(fs)
  
  data_dir <- path("data", "mp01")
    
  if(!dir_exists(data_dir)){
    dir_create(data_dir, showWarnings=FALSE, recurse=TRUE)
  }
  
  NETFLIX_URLS <- list(
    `2023H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-a-netflix-engagement-report",
    `2023H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2023",
    `2024H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2024",
    `2024H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2024",
    `2025H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2025",
    `2025H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2025",
    `2026H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2026"
  )
  
  download_if_needed <- function(url, local){
    if(!file_exists(local)){
      download.file(url, local, quiet = TRUE, mode = "wb")
    }
    
    return(local)
  }
    
  NETFLIX_URLS |> 
    map(read_html) |> 
    map(html_element, xpath = "//a[contains(@href, '.xlsx')]") |> 
    map_chr(html_attr, "href") |> 
    enframe("period", "url") |> 
   mutate(path = path(data_dir, paste0(period, ".xlsx")), 
          path = map2(url, path, download_if_needed), 
          data = map(path, read_excel, skip=3, na="*"), 
          data = map(data, \(x) x |> mutate(`Release Date` = as.Date(`Release Date`)))) |>
    unnest(data) |> 
    select(-url, -path) |> 
    mutate(year=str_sub(period, 1, 4), 
           half=str_sub(period, start=6), 
           period_start = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 1, 7), 1), 
           period_end = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 6, 12), if_else(half==1, 30, 31))) |> 
    filter(!is.na(`Hours Viewed`)) |> 
    mutate(live_event = !is.na(Runtime) & str_detect(Runtime, "\\d+:\\d+\\*"), 
           clean_time = str_remove(Runtime, fixed("*")),
           Runtime = 60 * hour(hm(clean_time)) + minute(hm(clean_time))) |>
    select(-clean_time) |>
    mutate(Runtime = coalesce(Runtime, mean(Runtime, na.rm=TRUE)), 
           .by=Title, 
           Title = stringi::stri_trans_general(Title, "Latin-ASCII"),
           Title = str_remove(Title, " // .*"), 
           Title = str_remove(Title, "[^[:ascii:]].*"), 
           Title = str_trim(Title)) |>
    filter(Title != "") |>
    select(year, 
           period, 
           period_start, 
           period_end, 
           Title, 
           -half, 
           Runtime, 
           Views, 
           `Hours Viewed`, 
           `Release Date`,
           `Available Globally?`, 
           live_event) |>
    rename(
      title = Title,
      runtime = Runtime,
      views = Views,
      hours_viewed = `Hours Viewed`,
      release_date = `Release Date`,
      available_globally = `Available Globally?`
    ) |>
    mutate(
      year= as.integer(year),
      available_globally = available_globally == "Yes"
    )
  

}

NETFLIX_DATA <- load_netflix_data()

Convert year from text like “2023” to an integer like 2023, so R can treat it as a number. Convert “Yes” / “No” in available_globally to logical TRUE / FALSE, so you can filter and count those values easily.
